Quick answer

Federal construction fall protection is generally required at 6 feet (29 CFR 1926.501). General industry walking-working surfaces are often 4 feet (1910.28). Scaffolds are commonly 10 feet. A body belt is not fall arrest. Cal/OSHA can be stricter — ask your competent person. This 15-question quiz is free practice, not a card.

What Outreach classes actually test

Falls are the leading killer in construction, so OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 classes drill trigger heights until they stick. The trap is mixing programs: a warehouse (general industry) is not a roof (construction), and a scaffold has its own number. If a question says “construction,” start at 6 feet unless the item is about scaffolds, steel erection, or another listed exception.

Personal fall arrest is a system: anchorage, full-body harness, and connecting device. Maximum free fall is generally 6 feet, and the anchorage is typically rated for 5,000 pounds or designed by a qualified person. Body belts are not used for fall arrest — they are a positioning tool, and using one to stop a fall is a classic wrong answer on practice items.

Hole covers must support twice the intended load, be secured, and be marked. Guardrails on construction typically have a top rail around 42 inches. California: Title 8 can set different heights and extra roofing rules. Confirm with your employer, then verify against OSHA fall protection and Cal/OSHA.

Common questions

When is fall protection required?

Federal construction: generally 6 feet. General industry: often 4 feet. Scaffolds: commonly 10 feet. Cal/OSHA may differ. Your competent person names the number for your site.

Is this an official OSHA test?

No. These are original practice questions. They are not copied from a DOL exam, and finishing the quiz does not issue an OSHA 10 or 30 card.

Who is this for?

Workers, apprentices, and people studying before an authorized Outreach class who want to review fall-protection numbers and jobsite decisions in plain language.

Where should I verify the rules?

Use OSHA’s fall-protection materials and 29 CFR 1926 Subpart M. Your employer’s program and an authorized trainer are the official path for a card.
