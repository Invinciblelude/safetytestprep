How to pass OSHA 30

Show up for the hours. Know the Focus Four and fall numbers. Ignore “real exam dump” PDFs. Then use practice to make the trainer’s test less of a surprise.

Quick answer

You pass OSHA 30 by completing 30 hours with an authorized Outreach trainer. OSHA does not publish one official final exam. This site’s 30-style mock is 50 questions, 35 to pass (70%). That score is practice. It is not a card.

Step 1 — The class is the exam

OSHA 30 is a 30-hour course, not a DMV-style permit test you can take in a kiosk. Authorized trainers track attendance. Miss hours and you do not get the card, even if you can recite 1926.501.

Use the OSHA Outreach Training Program page to understand who may issue a card. If a site sells a “card in 2 hours” or a PDF of “100 official answers,” walk away.

Step 2 — Study what actually kills people

Construction 30-hour classes spend the most time on the Focus Four because that is where fatalities cluster:

  • Falls — usual construction trigger 6 feet (29 CFR 1926.501); scaffolds often 10 feet; body belts are not fall arrest
  • Struck-by — vehicles, falling objects, flying tools
  • Caught-in/between — trenches (protective system by 5 feet in construction, unless in stable rock), unguarded equipment
  • Electrocution — overhead lines, damaged cords, missing GFCI, LOTO when servicing

Supervisors also get more time on how a program is supposed to run: inspections, competent person, recording injuries, and talking to a crew before a task. If you are taking General Industry 30, drill walking-working surfaces (often 4 feet), 1910.147 lockout/tagout, HazCom / SDS, and machine guarding instead of assuming every number is the construction 6-foot rule.
