What this quiz covers

  • Why falls are the leading killer in construction
  • Struck-by from vehicles, falling objects, and flying materials
  • Caught-in/between events such as trench collapse and unguarded equipment
  • Electrocution from overhead lines, damaged cords, and missing GFCI

Why this is on every OSHA 10 and 30

OSHA groups these four because that is where the bodies are. A 10-hour construction class will return to them in almost every module. A 30-hour class adds how a supervisor is supposed to stop the same events: inspections, competent person, and not waving people into an unprotected trench “for a minute.”

Falls still lead. Struck-by includes dump trucks, swinging loads, and powder-actuated tools. Caught-in/between is trench collapse and unguarded rotating equipment — in construction, a protective system is generally required at 5 feet unless you are in stable rock. Electrocution is overhead lines, missing GFCI on temporary power, and reaching into a panel that was never locked out.

Study tip: for each item, name which of the four it is before you pick A/B/C/D. Then drill fall protection, excavation, and electrical. For how this sits in a 10- versus 30-hour card path, read how to pass OSHA 30.
